# Cancer Basics

## What is Cancer?
Cancer is not a single disease, but a broad group of over 200 related diseases. At its core, cancer is fundamentally a disease of the cells. In a healthy body, human cells grow and divide to form new cells as the body needs them. When cells grow old or become damaged, they die, and new cells take their place. 

Cancer develops when this orderly process breaks down. As cells become more and more abnormal, old or damaged cells survive when they should die, and new cells form when they are not needed. These extra cells can divide without stopping and may form solid growths called tumors. Some cancers, like leukemia (cancer of the blood), generally do not form solid tumors.

## How it Develops
Cancer is caused by changes (mutations) to the DNA within cells. The DNA inside a cell is packaged into a large number of individual genes, each of which contains a set of instructions telling the cell what functions to perform, as well as how to grow and divide. Errors in these instructions can cause the cell to stop its normal function and may allow a cell to become cancerous. 
These genetic changes can be inherited from our parents, or they can arise during a person's lifetime as a result of errors that occur as cells divide or because of damage to DNA caused by certain environmental exposures (such as chemicals in tobacco smoke, or radiation like ultraviolet rays from the sun).

## The Hallmarks of Cancer
Researchers have identified several fundamental capabilities that cancer cells acquire as they develop, often referred to as the "Hallmarks of Cancer." These include:
1.  **Sustaining proliferative signaling:** Finding ways to continuously command themselves to grow and divide.
2.  **Evading growth suppressors:** Ignoring signals that normally tell cells to stop dividing.
3.  **Resisting cell death:** Evading apoptosis, the normal process of programmed cell death.
4.  **Enabling replicative immortality:** Maintaining the length of their telomeres, allowing them to divide infinitely without aging.
5.  **Inducing angiogenesis:** Stimulating the growth of new blood vessels to supply the tumor with oxygen and nutrients.
6.  **Activating invasion and metastasis:** Gaining the ability to break away from the primary tumor, travel through the blood or lymph system, and establish new tumors in other organs.
7.  **Evading immune destruction:** Hiding from or suppressing the body's immune system, which would normally destroy abnormal cells.

## Common Terminology
*   **Benign:** A tumor that is not cancerous. It can grow large but will not invade nearby tissues or spread to other parts of the body.
*   **Malignant:** A tumor that is cancerous. It can invade and destroy nearby tissue and spread to other parts of the body.
*   **Metastasis:** The spread of cancer cells from the place where they first formed to another part of the body. A metastatic cancer has the same name and same type of cancer cells as the original, or primary, cancer.
*   **Staging:** The process of finding out how much cancer is in a person's body and where it is located. It is essential for planning treatment and determining prognosis.
*   **Grading:** A measure of how abnormal the cancer cells look under a microscope. High-grade tumors look more abnormal and tend to grow faster than low-grade tumors.
*   **Remission:** A decrease in or disappearance of signs and symptoms of cancer. In partial remission, some, but not all, signs and symptoms of cancer have disappeared. In complete remission, all signs and symptoms have disappeared, although cancer still may be in the body.

## Treatment Modalities Overview
Cancer treatment is often multidisciplinary, involving a combination of therapies tailored to the specific type and stage of the disease.
*   **Surgery:** A procedure in which a surgeon removes cancer from the body. It is often used for solid tumors that are contained in one area.
*   **Chemotherapy:** The use of drugs to kill cancer cells, usually by stopping the cancer cells from keeping, growing or dividing. Because chemotherapy targets all rapidly dividing cells, it can also damage healthy cells, leading to side effects like hair loss and nausea.
*   **Radiation Therapy:** Uses high doses of radiation (like X-rays or protons) to kill cancer cells and shrink tumors by damaging their DNA.
*   **Immunotherapy:** A type of biological therapy that helps the patient's own immune system fight cancer. It works by either stimulating the immune system or providing man-made immune system proteins.
*   **Targeted Therapy:** A type of treatment that uses drugs or other substances to precisely identify and attack certain types of cancer cells based on specific genetic mutations or proteins, usually while doing less damage to normal cells.
*   **Hormone Therapy:** A treatment that slows or stops the growth of breast and prostate cancers that use hormones to grow.
