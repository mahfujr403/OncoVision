# Lung Squamous Cell Carcinoma

## Definition
Lung squamous cell carcinoma (SqCC) is a major subtype of Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC), accounting for roughly 25% to 30% of all lung cancer cases. It originates in the squamous cells—flat, scale-like cells that line the inside of the airways (bronchi). Because of its cellular origin, SqCC typically develops in the central part of the lungs, near the main air passages. It is very strongly associated with a history of tobacco smoking; it is uncommon in people who have never smoked.

## Histopathology Features
The diagnosis of SqCC relies on distinct microscopic features that differentiate it from other NSCLCs.
*   **Keratinization:** A hallmark feature is the presence of keratin pearls or intercellular bridges (desmosomes) connecting the tumor cells.
*   **Cellular Appearance:** The cells are often large with abundant cytoplasm and hyperchromatic (darkly staining) nuclei.
*   **Growth Pattern:** It often grows in solid nests or sheets. Central necrosis (tissue death) within the tumor masses is common and can lead to cavitation, which may be visible on chest X-rays or CT scans.
*   **Immunohistochemistry (IHC):** To distinguish SqCC from adenocarcinoma, pathologists use specific IHC markers. SqCC is typically positive for p40, p63, and Cytokeratin 5/6 (CK5/6), and negative for TTF-1 and Napsin A.

## Molecular Markers
Unlike lung adenocarcinoma, which frequently harbors actionable driver mutations (like EGFR or ALK), squamous cell carcinoma has a different molecular profile.
*   Actionable mutations are rare in pure SqCC. Routine testing for EGFR and ALK is generally not recommended unless the patient is a never-smoker or the biopsy sample is small and mixed histology cannot be ruled out.
*   Common genetic alterations in SqCC include mutations or amplifications in genes like FGFR1, PIK3CA, and PTEN, as well as mutations in the TP53 tumor suppressor gene.
*   **PD-L1 Expression:** Testing for Programmed Death-Ligand 1 (PD-L1) is crucial, as its expression level guides the use of immunotherapy.

## Treatment Options
Treatment strategies for SqCC are determined by the cancer stage and the patient's performance status.
*   **Surgery:** For early-stage (Stage I and II) disease, surgical resection (e.g., lobectomy) offers the best chance for a cure.
*   **Radiation and Chemotherapy:** For locally advanced (Stage III) disease that cannot be surgically removed, concurrent chemoradiotherapy is the standard approach.
*   **Immunotherapy:** Immune checkpoint inhibitors (like pembrolizumab, nivolumab, cemiplimab, atezolizumab) have revolutionized the treatment of advanced SqCC. They are frequently used as first-line therapy for metastatic disease, either as a single agent (monotherapy) for tumors with high PD-L1 expression, or in combination with chemotherapy regardless of PD-L1 levels.
*   **Chemotherapy:** Platinum-based chemotherapy doublets (e.g., cisplatin or carboplatin combined with gemcitabine or taxanes) remain a cornerstone of treatment, especially when combined with immunotherapy. Pemetrexed is NOT used for SqCC due to lack of efficacy compared to its use in adenocarcinoma.

## Differences from Adenocarcinoma
SqCC and Adenocarcinoma are the two main subtypes of NSCLC, but they differ significantly:
*   **Location:** SqCC tends to be centrally located near the main bronchi, while adenocarcinoma is typically found in the lung periphery.
*   **Smoking Association:** SqCC is almost exclusively tied to a history of smoking, whereas adenocarcinoma is the most common lung cancer in non-smokers.
*   **Molecular Profile:** Adenocarcinoma frequently has targetable mutations (EGFR, ALK, ROS1), allowing for targeted oral therapies. SqCC rarely has these mutations.
*   **Chemotherapy Choice:** Specific chemotherapy agents differ; for example, pemetrexed is effective for adenocarcinoma but not recommended for squamous cell carcinoma.
*   **Complications:** Due to its central location, SqCC is more likely to cause symptoms like airway obstruction, post-obstructive pneumonia, or coughing up blood (hemoptysis) earlier in the disease course compared to peripheral adenocarcinomas.
