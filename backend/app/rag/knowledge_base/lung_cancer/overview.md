# Lung Cancer Overview

## Introduction
Lung cancer is a type of cancer that begins in the lungs. It is the leading cause of cancer deaths worldwide. The lungs are two spongy organs in the chest that take in oxygen when you inhale and release carbon dioxide when you exhale. Lung cancer typically develops in the cells that line the air passages.

## Types of Lung Cancer
Doctors divide lung cancer into two major types based on the appearance of lung cancer cells under a microscope:

### Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)
NSCLC is an umbrella term for several types of lung cancers that behave in a similar way. It accounts for about 80% to 85% of lung cancers. The most common subtypes include:
*   **Adenocarcinoma:** Begins in the cells that secrete substances such as mucus. It occurs mainly in current or former smokers, but it is also the most common type of lung cancer seen in non-smokers. It is more common in women than in men, and it is more likely to occur in younger people than other types of lung cancer. It is usually found in outer parts of the lung.
*   **Squamous Cell Carcinoma:** Also known as epidermoid carcinoma. It forms in the squamous cells, which are flat cells that line the inside of the airways in the lungs. It is often linked to a history of smoking and tends to be found in the central part of the lungs, near a main airway (bronchus).
*   **Large Cell (Undifferentiated) Carcinoma:** Can appear in any part of the lung. It tends to grow and spread quickly, which can make it harder to treat.

### Small Cell Lung Cancer (SCLC)
SCLC, also known as oat cell cancer, occurs almost exclusively in heavy smokers and is less common than NSCLC, accounting for about 10% to 15% of all lung cancers. It tends to grow and spread faster than NSCLC.

## Epidemiology
Lung cancer is one of the most common cancers globally. It is the second most common cancer in both men and women in the United States (not counting skin cancer), but the leading cause of cancer death, making up almost 25% of all cancer mortalities.

## Risk Factors
Several factors may increase the risk of lung cancer:
*   **Smoking:** Tobacco use is by far the leading risk factor for lung cancer. About 80% of lung cancer deaths are thought to result from smoking. The risk increases with the number of cigarettes smoked daily and the number of years smoked.
*   **Secondhand Smoke:** Even for non-smokers, exposure to secondhand smoke increases the risk of lung cancer.
*   **Radon Gas:** Radon is a naturally occurring radioactive gas that can accumulate in homes and buildings. It is the second leading cause of lung cancer overall, and the leading cause among non-smokers.
*   **Asbestos and Other Carcinogens:** Workplace exposure to asbestos and other cancer-causing substances—such as arsenic, chromium, and nickel—can increase the risk, especially for smokers.
*   **Family History:** Individuals with a parent, sibling, or child with lung cancer have an increased risk.
*   **Air Pollution:** Chronic exposure to polluted air can slightly increase the risk.

## Symptoms
Lung cancer often does not cause signs and symptoms in its earliest stages. Signs and symptoms typically occur when the disease is advanced. Common symptoms include:
*   A new cough that doesn't go away
*   Coughing up blood (hemoptysis), even a small amount
*   Shortness of breath
*   Chest pain
*   Hoarseness
*   Unexplained weight loss
*   Bone pain
*   Headache

## Staging (TNM System)
Staging describes the extent or spread of the cancer at the time of diagnosis. It is crucial for determining the treatment plan and prognosis. The most common system for staging NSCLC is the TNM system:
*   **T (Tumor):** Refers to the size and extent of the main tumor.
*   **N (Nodes):** Indicates whether the cancer has spread to nearby lymph nodes.
*   **M (Metastasis):** Indicates whether the cancer has metastasized (spread) to other organs in the body (e.g., brain, bones, liver).

The TNM combination assigns a stage from 0 to IV:
*   **Occult (Hidden) Stage:** Cancer cells found in sputum, but tumor cannot be seen on imaging.
*   **Stage 0:** Cancer is found only in the top layers of cells lining the air passages (carcinoma in situ).
*   **Stage I:** The tumor is in the lung but has not spread to lymph nodes.
*   **Stage II:** The tumor is larger or has spread to nearby lymph nodes.
*   **Stage III:** Cancer has spread to lymph nodes in the center of the chest or other structures.
*   **Stage IV:** Cancer has spread to both lungs, to fluid around the lungs, or to other parts of the body (advanced disease).

SCLC uses a simpler two-stage system:
*   **Limited Stage:** Cancer is found on one side of the chest (one lung and nearby lymph nodes) and can be treated with a single radiation field.
*   **Extensive Stage:** Cancer has spread widely throughout the lung, to the other lung, to lymph nodes on the other side, or to other parts of the body.

## Survival Rates
Survival rates depend on the stage at diagnosis, the type of lung cancer, and the patient's overall health. 
For NSCLC, the 5-year relative survival rate for localized disease is approximately 63%, but drops to 8% for distant (metastasized) disease. 
For SCLC, the overall 5-year relative survival rate is much lower, around 7%, due to its aggressive nature and typical late-stage diagnosis. Early detection through screening (like low-dose CT scans) for high-risk individuals can significantly improve survival outcomes.
